Honeywell FC100A1029: size, cabinets, and what actually replaces it

OEM page · Updated Nov 5, 2026 · Dimensions checked against Honeywell/Resideo and retailer listings on that date

Quick answerFC100A1029 is Honeywell’s MERV 11 replacement media for the 16 × 25 F100 / F200 media air-cleaner cabinets (cabinet model F100F1625 / F200F1625). Honeywell’s own replacement table lists it at 16 × 24⅞ × 4⅜ inches. Retailers often call the same part nominal 16x25x4 (or shoppers call the cabinet a “16x25x5”); a standard big-box 16x25x4 that measures ~3⅝″ deep will leave a gap in this cabinet. Order by the part number on the old filter or cabinet door — not by the “x4” / “x5” nickname alone.

What the “x4 vs x5” label fight means

  • Depth is the trap. This media filter is about 4⅜″ deep. A “standard” 16x25x4 cut for a different cabinet family is often ~3.63–3.75″ deep and will sit loose, leaving unfiltered bypass around the edges.
  • Face size is close to 16 × 25. Honeywell writes 16 × 24⅞; the SupplyHouse Honeywell Home listing writes 15-15/16 × 24⅞. Either way, don’t order a 16x25x1 (¾″ deep) for this cabinet.
  • Trust the part number printed on the old cartridge or on the cabinet door over any “16x25x4/5” nickname in a shopping cart.

Where it’s installed
In the return-air duct, usually next to the blower compartment — not in the supply duct. The media cabinet is a dedicated thick rack with an access door; it is not a wall-grille 1″ slot.
Orientation
Slide the cartridge in so the airflow arrow points toward the furnace / blower (same direction as system airflow). Honeywell’s guide calls for at least 26″ clearance in front of the cabinet to pull the filter.

MERV describes particle-capture test performance, not medical outcomes. For a plain-language summary of what MERV bands mean, see the EPA MERV page. Ask an HVAC technician before moving from MERV 11 to MERV 13 if you’re unsure about blower capacity or duct design.

How often to change it

  • Honeywell’s guide: replace when pressure drop across the media reaches 0.5 in. w.c., or at least annually.
  • For optimum performance the same guide suggests about every six months (before heating and before cooling seasons), sooner if an atomizing humidifier is upstream or the system runs heating and cooling hard.
  • Record the install date on the filter frame if it has a space for it.

FC100A1029 fit checklist

  • Old filter or cabinet door shows FC100A1029, FC200E1029, or cabinet F100F1625 / F200F1625 (or the superseded CF100A1009).
  • Old filter tapes near 16 × 24⅞ with depth about 4⅜″ — not ~¾″ and not ~3⅝″.
  • New listing shows actual depth ≈ 4⅜″ (4.38″), not a thin 1″ or a shallow “x4” for a different cabinet.
  • Airflow arrow pointed toward the furnace / blower.
  • Access door closes fully; filter seats square with no crush or large edge gaps.
