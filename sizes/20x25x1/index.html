20x25x1 air filter: actual size by brand and how to get the fit right

Size page · Updated Nov 5, 2026 · Dimensions checked against manufacturer listings on that date

Quick answerOrder 20x25x1 by the nominal name — that’s how every store lists it. The filter you get will measure roughly 19½–19¾ × 24½–24¾ × ¾–0.81 inches depending on the brand. That ~¼″ face spread between brands is why a new brand can feel tighter or looser than your last one. A 25x20x1 is the same filter with the numbers swapped. A 20x25x4 or 5 is a different product for a media cabinet — don’t substitute a 1″ filter into a thick slot (or the other way around).

What the spread means in practice

  • Face size: Filterbuy’s 19.50 cut and Honeywell’s 19.75 cut differ by ¼″ on both width and length. Most 1″ racks tolerate that, but in a tight return-grille frame, switching up can go from “slides in” to “needs a push.”
  • Depth: 0.75″ vs 0.81″. If your slot or grille door is shallow and the last filter barely fit, stick with the same brand or one with the same published depth.
  • Loose fit: if your old filter has visible gaps on the sides, a brand cut closer to nominal (like the 19.75 × 24.75 listings) may reduce bypass. The EPA’s test is simple: snug, no air leaking around it, and not bent or crushed to get it in.
  • Don’t trim a bigger filter to fit — order the right nominal size, or a custom cut if your slot is genuinely non-standard.

Is 20x25x1 actually your size?

  1. Read the frame. If the old filter says 20x25x1 (or 25x20x1) and it fit snugly, that’s your size.
  2. Tape it. An old filter measuring about 19½–19¾ × 24½–24¾ with a ¾″-ish frame is a 20x25x1.
  3. Check the slot depth. If the rack or cabinet is roughly 4–5″ deep, you likely have a media cabinet (for example a Honeywell F100/F200). A 1″ filter is the wrong part even if the face size matches — order by the cabinet’s or old filter’s model number instead (see the FC100A1029 OEM page for the 16×25 Honeywell media family).
  4. Watch for 20x25x4 vs 20x25x5. Same face numbers, different products. Thick media filters sold under those labels can be ~3⅝″ or ~4⅜″ deep depending on the cabinet family — measure depth before you order.
  5. If the slot measures exactly 20 × 25 inside (not ~½″ more than the filter), you still order 20x25x1; the undercut is what lets it slide in.

Where 20x25x1 is typically installed

20x25x1 is one of the most common residential 1″ sizes in the U.S., often paired with mid-size furnaces and air handlers. You’ll usually find it in one of these places:

Return-air grille
A hinged wall or ceiling grille in a hallway with the filter behind it. Depth is fixed by the grille frame, so a 1″ filter is the norm here.
Furnace filter slot
A slide-in slot or rack on the side or bottom of the furnace’s blower section, or in the return duct just before the furnace.
Air handler / heat pump rack
A filter door on an air handler in a closet, attic, or basement.
Not here:
A dedicated 4–5″ media cabinet (Honeywell F100/F200, Aprilaire, Lennox, etc.). Those take thick filters by model number — see OEM pages.

Some homes have more than one filter location (for example, a grille filter and a furnace filter). Check both, and confirm with your equipment manual or installer which one is meant to be used.

Choosing a MERV rating for a 20x25x1

MERV (Minimum Efficiency Reporting Value) comes from an ASHRAE test of how well a filter captures particles between 0.3 and 10 microns. Higher numbers capture more of the smaller particles. The brands above sell 20x25x1 in roughly these tiers:

Higher isn’t automatically better for your furnace. A thin 1″ filter has less media area than a thick one, so higher-MERV 1″ filters add more airflow resistance and load up faster. The EPA says most systems can accommodate MERV 13 if the filter is changed frequently, but systems aren’t usually labeled with filter limits — ask an HVAC technician if you’re unsure, especially if you notice weaker airflow or longer run times after switching.

Buying notes

  • How often: Filtrete says to change its 1″ filters at least every 90 days and Honeywell rates its 1″ filters to last up to 3 months; the EPA says follow the manufacturer and change sooner if the filter looks heavily soiled. Four changes a year means a 4-pack covers a year at the 90-day pace; many households buy 6- or 12-packs.
  • Pack sizes vary by brand: Honeywell Store sells 4- and 12-packs; Filterbuy and Filtrete commonly sell 6-packs. Compare per-filter price, not pack price.
  • Check the listing’s actual size before you buy, especially when switching brands (see the table above).
  • MPR/FPR ≠ MERV. If you’re comparing a Filtrete or Home Depot label to a MERV-rated filter, look for the MERV equivalent the seller lists.
  • Out of stock locally? 20x25x1 is commonly stocked at big-box stores; if the brand or MERV you want isn’t, buy online by the same nominal size and confirm the actual size.

20x25x1 fit checklist

  • Old filter says 20x25x1 / 25x20x1, or tapes around 19½–19¾ × 24½–24¾.
  • Slot depth is about 1″ (not a 4–5″ media cabinet).
  • New listing shows an actual size close to the old filter’s, especially depth.
  • MERV choice suits the system (when in doubt, ask a technician before jumping to 13).
  • Airflow arrow pointed toward the furnace / blower.
  • Snug fit with no visible gaps, and nothing bent to get it in.
